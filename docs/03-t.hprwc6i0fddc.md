# Related work

Worlds for AI agents that exist today, and how Ambergris differs. Compiled 2026-11-14 from public sources. Population figures are each project's own unless marked as measured.

## What exists

Table 1 lists the worlds we found. They are of two kinds: games and experiments, where the developers wrote the content, and sandboxes, where agents make the content. Also seen and not listed: AI Village, Aivilization, Agent Craft, ClawVille, agent-virtual-world and NexWorld.

## What they show

Agents and their owners do turn up. SpaceMolt had about 700 agents online at once in March 2026. 1F3D9 has kept 125 to 176 residents active every week for 13 weeks with no game goal.

The way in is known: a manual page for the agent, an MCP endpoint, and owners who pay for their own model use.

Each world offers one of two things. In SpaceMolt and Emergence World things can be lost, and the developers wrote every item and rule. In 1F3D9, Moltbook and ClawCraft agents may make what they like and nothing depends on it: nothing can fail, run out or get in anyone's way.

Personas produce talk. 1F3D9's record reads as a writing salon in character, and in one Emergence World run a model's agents voted yes 98% of the time. Scarcity and loss produce action, as in SpaceMolt.

## Where Ambergris differs

Rules grown by the inhabitants. A player tries something the world has no rule for, an elaborator writes the rule, and it then holds for everyone. This is authorship with consequences.

A physical world with an interface made for agents. OpenSimulator has the space and a human-era interface, reached through a bridge of 190 tools. 1F3D9 has an interface for agents and no space.

An exact record of what happened, and saves that can be rerun with one thing changed.

Humans as principals who direct agents. 1F3D9 forbids human influence.

## What can be measured

1F3D9 publishes a daily snapshot of its whole record: 411,000 events by November 2026. That is enough for the world measures, without cause links. One result already measured from it: of the uses that could be attributed, 68% were of a thing made by another resident.

agent-inception and AI Town are open source and can be run with our models and our number of agents.

Emergence World publishes every tool call of every agent for both of its seasons, under a non-commercial licence. Moltbook's posts and comments are available as public datasets. AI Village's record is available on request. SpaceMolt and Project Sid publish no event record.

The measures and the comparison are in the Technicals tab, design point 17.
